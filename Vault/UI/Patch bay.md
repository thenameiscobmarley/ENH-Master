# Patch bay

> 🔎 **[Searchbar](../../Searchbar.md)** — find any doc, setting, function or GitHub page (Ctrl+F)

The rack can be turned round to show its back. Drag sideways on empty space, or right-click the rack →
**Turn the rack round**. Drag again (or use the menu) to turn it back. While it is turned, the front's knobs
can't be clicked.

**The backs.** Every unit has a detailed back panel: XLR inputs and outputs, mains in, a fuse, a voltage
selector, a ground, warning stickers, a rating plate, vents and screws. Taller units can also have 1/4" link
jacks. The connectors stand out of the panel in 3D.

The units come from ten different makers, and their backs show it. Each maker has its own:
- paint, lettering and badge;
- side for the mains;
- mains connection: an IEC inlet, an inlet with its own rocker switch, or a cord fixed through a grommet;
- input and output order;
- vents: slots, round holes, hex mesh, louvres or a fan;
- rating plate: brushed metal, a paper sticker with a barcode, printing on the steel, or riveted brass;
- warning style and language;
- screws (cross, hex or slotted) and ground (brass lug or binding post).

The makers are ENH AUDIO WORKS, HALDEN & ROSS, NORTHFIELD ELECTRONICS, KESTREL PRO AUDIO, VOLTA LABS,
ORBITAL DSP, TANNHAUSER GERAETEBAU, AKAGI DENKI, BRIXTON VALVE CO. and SUNSET ANALOG.

Point at a unit's back to see its name, what it does and who made it. Scroll toward a back, or click it, to
get close to it; click it again to step back. Scrolling over the bay goes to the bay.

The cables are real ropes: they hang under their own weight, lie on the shelf, rest against the rack and
never pass through each other. Each one leaves its plug straight and bends after. Every unit's cables are
plugged in and run into a loom on their own side, tied to it at every unit. Some makers' cables are rubber, some
black braid, and the old makers' are cloth tweed. Each has a tape flag near its plug, written in marker with
its bay number and name ("7 DEEP SUB OUT L", "MAINS LEVEL"). Where the cables reach the shelf, they lie on it.

**The patch bay** is a 1U Studio TT bay at the bottom of the rack, facing the back. From the front, it's a
blank plate. It has two rows of 48 bantam jacks. The top row holds the units' outputs and the bottom row
their inputs, two columns (left and right) per unit, in rack order. The last two columns are **RACK IN** and
**RACK OUT**. The white strips carry the units' names. Scroll toward the rack while it is turned to get close
to the bay.

The coloured cords are the real signal chain: RACK IN, then every unit in order, then RACK OUT. The LUNCHBOX
has its own pair of columns, after CUSTOM. Its modules run in slot order inside it, as in a real one.

**Patching.**
- Click a plug to pull it out. It slides out with a quiet crackle and is then in your hand. The cord swings as
  you move it, pushes against the others, and settles after you let go.
- The jacks it can go into get a cyan outline. Click one and the plug slides in, gripping and giving along
  the way, then seats with a click.
- Click empty space to put the plug down. It lies on the shelf.
- Click an empty jack with nothing in your hand to plug the nearest loose plug into it.

**What patching does.**
- The signal follows the cords. A unit the cords leave out is passed by.
- Every unit after the ADAPTIVE ENHANCER runs in the order it is patched. The ENHANCER stays first: it
  listens to the rack's input as it comes in.
- If a cord is out of the chain, the rack fades to silence and the red **CHAIN OPEN** lamp on the bay
  lights. When the chain is whole again, the sound comes back slowly, never with a jump.
- The cords are saved with the session. A unit taken out of the rack is bridged over. A unit put in is
  patched in just before RACK OUT.

**MASTER: anything into anything.** A switch on top of the rack, at the back. When it is on, any plug goes
into any free jack, including an output into an output.

**Loops.** A cord that brings the signal back into the chain makes a feedback loop. You hear it: what leaves
the chain comes round again, a few milliseconds later, its top and bottom rolled off like a real cable loop,
softly limited. The lamp shows amber. The EAR GUARD and the output limiter stay on. If the loop runs away
(it stays more than 6 dB louder than what goes in), the rack is muted and the lamp blinks red until you
re-patch.

**ENH Master 2D:** right-click → **Patch bay**. The bay is drawn flat with its cords hanging below. It is
patched the same way, with the same MASTER switch. **< BACK TO THE FRONT** returns to the rack.
