DSP board24-Band Analyser
24 overlapping log-spaced bands on the mono input, with fast, short and medium followers and rolling statistics (noise floor, mean, deviation) for self-tuning.
DSP board4096-Pt Fft
The precision layer's ears: a 4096-point FFT (hop 2048) folded onto a 1/24-octave grid; what sticks up is a resonance, what sinks is a hole, and each one's width becomes a bell's Q.
DSP boardHarmonic Planner
Decides where harmonics are missing: a DEPTH source (70–500 Hz) and a CLARITY source (600–5000 Hz), whose centres glide with the music.
DSP boardAdaptive Eq
No target curve: each band follows its own target through a PD controller, and the 24 overlapping bells are solved by least squares so they don't pile up.
DSP boardPrecision Eq
Up to eight bells with their own frequency, Q and depth that follow resonances and holes as they drift. Cuts up to 8 dB, lifts up to 3; zero latency.
DSP boardSub Enhancer
A dynamic low shelf under a PD controller, and the sub band saturated and band-passed around its own 2nd and 3rd harmonics.
DSP boardHarmonic Exciter
Two exciters (DEPTH and CLARITY): the planned band isolated, normalised, and fed through Chebyshev polynomials T2 and T3, so only new 2nd and 3rd harmonics are added.
DSP boardAuto Gain
Loudness-matched auto gain, so the enhancement is heard as detail rather than as level.
DSP boardSoft Ceiling
Transformer/valve colour, DC blocking and a soft ceiling at about −0.2 dBFS, still inside the 2× oversampled section.
FaceplateFaceplate
The dark Pro-XL-style front: outlined sections with titles, printed scales, square push buttons with an LED above.

What it does for you

Hear more of the game. Hear your mix as it is.

One rack, two jobs. In a game it brings the detail forward and keeps the bangs in their place; in a DAW it is a set of adaptive mastering tools you can A/B at the same loudness.

Gamers

  • Every step, near and far. The FOOTSTEP RADAR lifts steps in their own bands; far ones get a little room, near ones stay dry.
  • Bangs stop ducking everything. A bass hit is taken down where it is, so the footsteps and voices above it keep their level.
  • Never too loud. EAR GUARD holds a sudden blast to 12 dB over what you've been hearing, and nothing leaves over 0 dBFS.
  • Between your games and your headset. The Windows app inserts the rack and takes it out again.
See the radar at work →

Producers

  • VST3 for any DAW on Windows or Linux.
  • Level-matched A/B. COMPARE plays the input at the output's loudness, so you judge the sound, not the level.
  • An EQ that listens. CLARITY builds its curve from the sound itself, and shows you every band it moves.
  • Consoles, tape and valves. CHARACTER blends two of nine models; TONE & SPACE adds polish, air, width and room.

Demo 1 / 4Clarity

An EQ that listens, and shows you what it's doing.

There's no fixed "smile" curve. CLARITY builds its curve from the sound itself, and you can watch it live over the spectrum.

  • 24 adaptive bands rebalance treble and bass against the midrange, but only when the sound really needs it.
  • Up to 8 precision bands, each with its own frequency, width (Q) and depth: a narrow dip where something rings, a wide one for a build-up, a gentle lift into a hole. You can watch every one move.
  • Harmonics add definition and body where they're missing, never at a fixed spot.

Try it: hide a ring, and watch CLARITY find it

We hid a +10 dB resonance at 2.3 kHz (Q 8) in pink noise. One precision band found it on its own, and cut exactly there, exactly that narrow: in the real test 2305 Hz, Q 8.9, cut 5.3 dB. Move the ring, make it a hole, or turn CLARITY, and see where the band goes.

The plugin's own maths. The band is placed by a line-by-line port of PrecisionEQ.cpp: the 1/24-octave grid, the baseline it is measured against, the width where it falls to half its height, the 8 dB cut and 3 dB lift limits scaled by CLARITY, and the narrower-cut rule between 250 Hz and 5 kHz. It is drawn with the exact response of its state-variable bell. Simplified: the noise is its long-term average (no randomness), and you see where the band settles, not its glide there.

What it showed: close footsteps in a loud mix are as loud and as broad as a gunshot, and the radar took them for shots. The difference is the shape: a shot is top-heavy, 10 to 15 dB stronger in the high bands; a footstep lands with weight, 3 to 9 dB stronger low down. The radar now tells them apart by that, keeps a walker's beat on the first hit of each step (a game's heel and toe), and stops treating a step that lands on the walker's beat as a rattle. Every check on the test bench still passes.

Demo 3 / 4Safe for your ears

Always on, whatever you turn.

EAR GUARD never lets the sound get more than 12 dB (about four times) louder than it has been. It measures loudness the way ears do (K-weighted, over 400 ms) and looks 5 ms ahead, so a blast after a quiet stretch is held down before you hear it.

  • the blast going in
  • what reaches your ears
  • guard settings
  • A +35 dB blast comes out at +13.7 dB. Loud-and-steady material, like a mix that is simply loud, is left untouched.
  • Want more punch? Set it to 15 or 18 dB. It can't be switched off.
  • Never over 0 dBFS, including the peaks between samples (true peak).
  • No pops when audio starts, and silence instead of a blast if anything ever goes wrong inside.
  • No DC or rumble below 8 Hz reaches your speakers.

Every one of these is checked by the automated tests on every build. Your headset's volume is still yours to set: start low.

Loudest 400 ms after it starts: +35.0 dB going in, +13.7 dB reaching your ears (held down by up to 23.9 dB).

The plugin's own maths: a line-by-line port of EarGuard.h, run at 48 kHz: the 400 ms energy budget, the "usual" loudness (learned for 2 s, then followed over 10 s up and 30 s down, rising at most 0.75 dB a second while it holds), the 5 ms look-ahead, the even spending (a burst at most 8 dB over the allowed level after a quiet stretch, none once the window is full), 0.8 ms attack, 60 ms release and 30 dB floor. Measured as the tests do: the loudest 400 ms in the first 2 s, against the 2 s before. Simplified: the sound is steady noise, so the guard sees only its K-weighted power, and the output limiter after it is left out. The test through the whole rack measures the same +13.7 dB.

Demo 4 / 4Character & Tone

Consoles, tape and valves, and a finish that shines.

CHARACTER blends two of nine hardware models (British and American consoles, class-A, tape at 15 and 30 ips, a breathing valve, and two tuned for games and film), with COLOUR at any volume and DRIVE for grit. TONE & SPACE adds polish, air, width and room.

Try it: a 1 kHz tone through CHARACTER

Pick a model and turn COLOUR and DRIVE. The bars are the harmonics it adds to a pure tone, against the tone itself: even ones sound warm and full, odd ones give edge. COLOUR brings the character in at any level; DRIVE pushes into grit.

Check your download

Make sure it's the real one.

  1. Every release has a SHA256SUMS.txt with a fingerprint of each download and of every script (build.sh, convert-to-windows.bat, the scripts/ folder), and a HOW-TO-CHECK.txt.
  2. Each zip also has a CHECKSUMS.txt for the files inside it, the scripts' fingerprints and the same HOW-TO-CHECK.txt. A zip can't hold its own fingerprint, so check the zip itself against the release page.
  3. Work out the fingerprint of your copy and compare it with the list. If even one character differs, don't run it.

Windows (PowerShell)

Get-FileHash .\ENH-Master-*-windows-gamer-app.zip -Algorithm SHA256

Windows (Command Prompt)

certutil -hashfile ENH-Master-...-windows-gamer-app.zip SHA256

Linux / macOS

sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS.txt --ignore-missing
